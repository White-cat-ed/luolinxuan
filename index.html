About Me

Research

My master’s research follows one coherent goal: generalized open underwater perception. I decompose it into environmental openness and category openness, address each challenge progressively, and then integrate the resulting capabilities.

01

Robust perception · 2024–2025

Prompt-UIE & WCPM

Parameter-efficient visual prompting for cross-water enhancement, followed by frequency–scale coupled feature learning for lightweight underwater detection.

Prompt-UIE uses only 0.88M trainable parameters; WCPM reaches 69.9 AP on DUO with 9.854M parameters.

02

Physics-guided domain generalization · 2024–2025

Hy-UOD

Models atmospheric light and transmission as complementary physical modalities, then uses a hypernetwork and multi-frequency adaptation to generate water-condition-aware detector parameters.

First-author work published at ACM MM 2025 Oral.

03

Generalized domain generalization · 2025–present

Hy-GUOD

Extends robust perception to sparse single-source supervision through physics-consistent domain evolution, inverse optical-state estimation, dynamic detector modulation, and conflict-aware bilevel optimization.

First-author manuscript in preparation.

Fine-grained open benchmark · 2025–2026

A five-level biological taxonomy with detection, instance segmentation, and open-vocabulary protocols for real-world aquatic biodiversity perception.

77,970 images · 16,540 species · 132,885 instances; first-author work published at KDD 2026.

KDD2026

KDD 2026 · CCF-A · Jeju, Republic of Korea

OpenAqua: A Large-Scale Fine-Grained Dataset and Benchmark for Open Underwater Visual Perception

Linxuan Luo, Pan Mu*, and Cong Bai

First author · Dataset and benchmark lead

Publisher PDF Project & Code Dataset

TL;DR: A five-level biological taxonomy and open underwater benchmark with 77,970 images, 16,540 species, and 132,885 fine-grained instances.

Abstract

Monitoring aquatic biodiversity is vital for maintaining global ecological balance. While advancements in computer vision have revolutionized underwater perception, existing datasets are predominantly limited to coarse-grained categories or lack spatial localization annotations, severely constraining the applicability of models for fine-grained biological identification in real-world scenarios. To address this gap, we introduce OpenAqua, the first large-scale fine-grained dataset dedicated to open underwater visual tasks. OpenAqua is structured around a five-level biological taxonomic hierarchy, comprising 77,970 high-quality images covering 16,540 aquatic species, and providing 132,885 fine-grained bounding boxes and corresponding instance segmentation masks. Based on this dataset, we establish a comprehensive benchmark suite that encompasses standard object detection, instance segmentation, and an underwater open-vocabulary object detection benchmark. Extensive evaluations reveal substantial performance degradation as current models progress from coarse-grained to fine-grained recognition, highlighting the challenges of fine-grained semantic perception and domain adaptation in degraded underwater environments.

ACM MMOral · 2025

ACM MM 2025 · CCF-A · Oral · pp. 2284–2293

Physics-Coupled Frequency Dynamic Adaptation Network for Domain Generalized Underwater Object Detection

Linxuan Luo, Pan Mu*, and Cong Bai

First author

Publisher PDF Code

TL;DR: Hy-UOD couples underwater imaging priors with multi-frequency dynamic adaptation for robust cross-water detection.

Abstract

Despite technological progress in underwater object detection, domain shift from diverse environmental conditions and severe image degradation remain major challenges. We propose Hy-UOD, a hypernetwork-powered domain generalization framework that synergizes physical priors with multi-frequency feature learning. A meta-learning hypernetwork synthesizes domain-generalization parameters by encoding environment-specific physical descriptors. A Multi-frequency Feature Dynamic Adaptation module then compensates for degraded features through low-frequency correction, high-frequency refinement, and mid-frequency reconstruction. Experiments on multiple underwater datasets demonstrate robust detection performance and strong cross-domain generalization.

ICASSP2025

ICASSP 2025 · CCF-B · Hyderabad, India · pp. 1–5

Prompt-UIE: A Unified Prompt-Driven Framework for Underwater Image Enhancement

Yanling Zhang, Linxuan Luo, Pan Mu*, and Cong Bai

Second author

Publisher PDF Code

TL;DR: Visual prompts and a physics-guided loss adapt one enhancement model to diverse underwater conditions with only 0.88M trainable parameters.

Abstract

Complex and diverse underwater environments cause multiple types of image degradation. Most existing methods focus on a single dataset, limiting the model’s ability to explore different degradation characteristics. Prompt-UIE adapts a pretrained task-specific model to varied underwater conditions without requiring multiple datasets. It combines a background-light visual prompt module with a reverse-transmission-map-guided loss: the prompt guides enhancement for different water types, while the loss improves the handling of spatially non-uniform degradation. Experiments on full-reference and no-reference datasets demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the method.
